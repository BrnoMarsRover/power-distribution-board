Status: **draft for review**. Branch `hardware/rev3`. Replaces board v2.4.0.

This is the plan for the next board, written before the schematic so the decisions can
be argued about while they are still cheap. Items marked **OPEN** need a team decision;
each has a recommended default (section 10).

Per-rail component values, the PTC choice per output and the I/O map are generated by
[`rails.py`](rails.py) into [`rails.md`](rails.md). The cost comparison behind the
architecture is generated by [`cost.py`](cost.py) into [`cost.md`](cost.md).

## 1. What rev2 taught us

Team notes on v2.4.0:

- Branch switching is unreliable, and sometimes a whole branch refuses to switch. Most
  FETs ended up bridged so the rover would start reliably.
- Adding dV/dt inrush limiting to the LT1910 stages helped, but did not fix it.
- The 5 V aux supply is hard to get working on some boards.
- OCP is disabled in firmware, because bridged branches cannot be opened.

A review of the rev2 schematic, layout and firmware found the following. They are ordered by
how well each explains the symptoms.

1. **The aux buck is miswired.** IC7 (MAX17541G) pin 4 is VCC, its internal 5 V LDO
   output. Pin 5 is FB (0.9 V). The two are tied together in both the schematic and the PCB.
   FB therefore sits near 5 V, so the regulator believes the output is far too high and does
   not switch.
   - The Pico then runs only when USB from the PC back-feeds VSYS through its internal diode.
   - The PC is powered *through* this board, so a cold start can deadlock: the board waits
     for the PC to power it over USB, and the PC waits for the board to switch its power on.
   - Bridging the FETs breaks that loop. This is the leading explanation for both "aux
     supply finicky" and "switching refuses to work".
   - *Check on a rev2 board:* battery only, USB unplugged, measure TP3.3. If it is not
     about 5 V, this is the cause.
2. **The inrush fix moved the stress into the FETs.** Each slew network is 100 kΩ plus
   1 µF to GND, charged by the LT1910's small internal charge pump, so every output ramps up
   slowly.
   - Downstream converters start at their UVLO, partway up the ramp, and draw full power
     while the FET still has tens of volts across it.
   - That is linear-mode dissipation outside the FET's SOA.
   - Nothing trips, because the current itself is normal.
3. **The LT1910 has no source pin.** At turn-off it pulls the gate to *ground*. Downstream
   capacitors keep the output up, so V_GS ≈ −V_out: about −42 V on Q1 and −24 V on Q5,
   against a ±20 V rating. The gate oxide takes a hit at every turn-off.
4. **Branch drivers are powered from the rail they switch.** Switching onto a capacitive
   load with a 60 W converter behind it can push the converter into current limit. The rail
   then collapses and the driver resets. The LT1910 needs ≥ 8 V, so the 12 V rail has only
   4 V of margin.
5. **The firmware makes the board fail-off.**
   - All five enables go high in the same instant.
   - A Pico reset drops every output.
   - Boot waits up to 10 s for USB.
   - If no INA238 answers, nothing is ever switched on.

Design rules for rev3, one per lesson:

| #  | Rule |
|----|------|
| R1 | Logic power never depends on USB or on any switched output. Bring-up test: the board boots on battery alone. |
| R2 | Every switch has a source-referenced gate drive and a designed inrush, checked against the FET's SOA. |
| R3 | Protection lives in hardware and acts without the MCU: a fast eFuse per rail, and a PTC per output for cable faults too small to trip the rail. |
| R4 | Rail states survive an MCU reset. Each rail's power-on default is set in hardware. |
| R5 | Everything on the battery bus is rated ≥ 80 V (100 V preferred). |
| R6 | All MCU I/O is 3.3 V. Nothing pulls a GPIO to 5 V. |

## 4. Power path

All values per rail are in [`rails.md`](rails.md).

### 4.1 Battery input

- **Connector:** XT90, male on the board, because the battery is the source.
- **Fuse:** a 58 V-rated fuse in the pack or harness, sized to the harness, not on the
  board. Standard ATO blades are rated 32 V DC and are not suitable on this bus.
- **TVS:** SMCJ45A (1500 W, 45 V standoff, 72.7 V clamp) at the connector.
- **Bulk capacitance:** 2 × 100 µF / 100 V Panasonic EEU-FC2A101 (radial 12.5 × 20 mm,
  through-hole for hand assembly) and 4 × 2.2 µF / 100 V X7R 1210.
- **Load (decided 2026-11-19):** about 300 W average from the battery, so about 8 A. The
  board's ratings (30 A per 42 V group) are for peaks and faults.
- **Battery monitor (decided 2026-11-19):** **INA228** with a 2 mΩ Bourns
  CSS2H-2512K-2L00F.
  - **INA228 over the INA238:** it accumulates charge and energy on chip, so nothing is
    lost between ESP32 reads or across an ESP32 reset while +3V3 stays up. Its 20-bit ADC
    gives 0.16 mA steps at this shunt. Same pinout and address (0x40) as the INA238.
  - **Shunt:** the CSS2H-2512K-2L00F is a 5 W, ±1 % part, with 5× overload for 5 s.
    - 2 mΩ gives 16 mV at the 8 A average. 0.5 mΩ would give 4 mV, and its solder joints
      would add comparable resistance.
    - Losses are 0.13 W at 8 A and 3.2 W at 40 A. A drive-group fault trip (about 83 A
      total for 0.5 s) is about 14 W, inside the 25 W / 5 s overload rating.
  - **Kelvin connection:** Bourns' recommended 4-terminal land pattern, drawn as a net-tie
    footprint (`R_Bourns_CSS2H-2512_Kelvin`). The sense fingers meet the current pads
    exactly where the resistive element starts.
  - **ADCRANGE = 0** (±163.84 mV): ±82 A full scale, enough for any fault.
  - **Rev2 replaced a 3 mΩ shunt and INA238 on the 42 V group branch only.** Rev3 sees
    the whole battery, including charging.
- **Sourcing:** every part comes from TME or Mouser (decided 2026-11-19). Schematic
  symbols carry the manufacturer part number in an `MPN` field.
- **Charging through the board:** both pack connectors land on the board (power XT90 and
  the pack's charge port), plus a charger input.
  - Charger + enters on the bus side of the shunt and leaves through the pack charge port
    on the battery side. The INA228 therefore counts charging while the rover runs: it
    sees the net battery current, charger output minus rover load.
  - Charger − (CHG_N) is not board GND. It runs straight to the charge port's − (C−), so
    a split-port BMS keeps its charge FET in the path. On a common-port pack, C− and P−
    are the same node anyway.
  - A swapped plug is harmless: the two charge connectors differ only by the 2 mΩ
    shunt, so swapping them only skips the measurement.
  - Assumes the BMS switches the − side (common +), which is the usual 10S layout. A pack
    with separate C+ and P+ must not have them joined on the board.
- **Before the 42 V group switches:** the aux supply and the battery monitor sit upstream
  of them, so the board stays alive and measuring with every output off.

### 4.2 The eFuse (all five switches)

All five switches (42 V drives, 42 V converter feeds, 24 V, 12 V, 5 V) are the same circuit: a **TPS48111-Q1** smart high-side driver, a main N-FET,
a pre-charge N-FET and a shunt.

| Feature | How it is used |
|---------|----------------|
| Gate drive | 12 V charge pump referenced to the FET source, so V_GS is correct at both turn-on and turn-off. This fixes rev2 lessons 3 and 4. |
| Pre-charge | A second FET ramps the output with a gate-slew capacitor (C_g) at about 2 A. The main FET only closes once the output is up, so it never runs in linear mode. |
| Over-current (IWRN) | Trips at about 1.1 × the rail's design current after a 0.5 s timer, then **latches off**. Firmware decides retries by toggling INP. |
| Short-circuit (ISCP) | 1.2 µs cut-off. Used on the switches fed from the battery (both 42 V groups and the 54 V supply); see 4.3. |
| Current monitor (IMON) | Analog, from the same shunt that trips the switch: ±2 % at 30 mV across the shunt, ±5 % at 6 mV, plus a ±0.2 mV offset (about ±0.1 A on a 2 mΩ shunt). |
| Faults | FLT_I and FLT_T, wire-ORed into one line per rail. |
| FET temperature | DIODE input, with an MMBT3904 placed against the main FET. |

The driver runs from 3.5 to 80 V (100 V absolute maximum), so the same part covers the 5 V
rail and the 42 V groups. Every switch FET is the same part: **IPB042N10N3G** (100 V,
4.2 mΩ, D2PAK, about $0.59). The 42 V groups use two in parallel.

### 4.3 Per-switch settings

| Switch | FETs | Shunt | Over-current trip | Short-circuit trip |
|--------|------|-------|-------------------|--------------------|
| 42 V drives (3 × XT90), 30 A design | 2 × IPB042N10N3G in parallel + 1 pre-charge | 0.5 mΩ | 60 A for 1.1 s (firmware: 20 A sustained) | 150 A |
| 42 V converter feeds (2 × XT90), 30 A | 2 × IPB042N10N3G in parallel + 1 pre-charge | 1 mΩ | 30 A for 0.5 s | 100 A |
| 24 V (8 × Deans T), 10 A | IPB042N10N3G main + pre-charge | 2 mΩ | 11.1 A | off |
| 12 V (7 × XT60), 10 A | IPB042N10N3G main + pre-charge | 2 mΩ | 11.1 A | off |
| 5 V (11 × XT30), 15 A | IPB042N10N3G main + pre-charge | 1 mΩ | 16.6 A | off |
| 54 V supply (boost input), 4 A | IPB042N10N3G main + pre-charge | 3 mΩ | 5.0 A | 20 A |

- **42 V groups, one direction:** the parallel FETs all point the same way, not
  back-to-back, so regenerative braking and back-driven motors always have a path back to
  the battery through the body diodes.
- **Converter-group trip: 30 A** (30 mV across 1 mΩ, inside TI's characterized 10–30 mV).
  That is 1.36× the 22 A the three converters draw flat-out at 30 V.
- **Power-on sequence (decided 2026-11-19): the ESP32 boots the rails.**
  - Every eFuse powers up OFF in hardware (100 k pull-downs on INP and INP_G).
  - The ESP32 runs from the always-on aux supply and boots in about 0.3–0.5 s. It brings
    up the boot rails in order, the converter group, then 12 V (Orin), then 54 V. It moves
    on as soon as each step is confirmed, which puts the Orin on power about 1.5–2.5 s after
    the battery connects.
  - Per rail:
    1. INP_G on.
    2. Wait for the pre-charge: VSNS_OUT within 5 % of the input.
    3. INP on, then INP_G off.
  - Once the converter group's main FETs are on, firmware clears BOOST_DIS and the 54 V
    boost soft-starts (about 12 ms). Until then the Zyxel sees the group's voltage minus a
    diode drop, through the boost's inductor and high-side body diode.
  - The 12 V rail starts only once the converter group is fully on, so nothing downstream
    draws current through the converter group's pre-charge FET.
  - A step that does not finish in about 4× its expected time, or an asserted FLT, leaves
    that rail off and is reported.
  - **Trade-off:** with dead firmware, the Orin and the network stay off. The TCA9535 keeps
    its outputs through an ESP32 reset, so a firmware crash or watchdog reset does not
    drop rails that are already on.
  - **Replaces** a hardware RC chain (1.5–21 s worst case) and, before that, a 1 MΩ / 1 µF
    INP delay that could close the main FETs before pre-charge had finished.
- **Emergency bypass bridges (decided 2026-11-19, made smaller the same day).** Every eFuse
  has two plated holes, one on its drain rail (after the shunt) and one on its output. In
  an emergency, solder a short solid-copper wire across them.
  - **Sizes:** 2.5 mm² wire in 2.3 mm holes 7.62 mm apart on the two 42 V groups; 1.5 mm²
    wire in 1.8 mm holes 5.08 mm apart on the 24 / 12 / 5 V rails and the 54 V supply.
    The bolt-on M4 / M3 links they replace took about four times the board area.
  - **What a link does:** the rail is powered whatever the TPS48111, its FETs or the
    firmware are doing. The shunt stays in the path, so IMON still reads the current.
  - **What a link gives up:**
    - That rail loses its overcurrent and short-circuit protection. Only the pack fuse
      remains.
    - It loses its inrush limit: bolting the link on, or plugging a device in, sparks.
      Fit the link with the battery off.
    - **On the drive group, the rear motor switch no longer cuts the motors.** The
      battery E-STOP (BMS soft-start) still does.
  - **Firmware flags it:** a rail with VSNS_OUT up while INP and INP_G are low is
    reported as BYPASSED.
  - The links are only for getting the rover home or debugging a dead switch. Remove them
    before normal use.
- **FLT pull-ups** (open-drain FLT_I + FLT_T on each eFuse) sit on the MCU sheet next to the
  ESP32 inputs, which keeps the eFuse sheets' control area clear.
- **The converter group** feeds about 22 A at 30 V (300 + 150 + 150 W of converters) through
  two XT90s; one of them is Y-split in the harness for the third converter. It is sized for
  30 A.
  Splitting it from the drives means a motor surge can no longer trip the Orin's supply.
- **42 V short-circuit trips stay on,** because the battery can deliver hundreds of amps.
- **Drive-group trip (decided 2026-11-19): hardware 60 A for 1.1 s, firmware 20 A
  sustained.**
  - An EPOS4 50/5 may draw 15 A for up to 15 s while accelerating (maxon hardware
    reference, table 3-12), so four drives could reach 60 A. The hardware trip sits at
    that level, with 30 mV across the shunt, the top of TI's characterized 10–30 mV range.
  - The ESP32 reads IMON_42D and drops INP_42D when the average stays above 20 A for
    several seconds (window to tune on the rover). At about 300 W for the whole rover, 20 A
    sustained on the drives means something is stuck or stalled.
- **Shunts are Bourns CSS2H-2512 on the Kelvin land pattern** (the same net-tie footprint
  as the battery shunt), R_SET is fed from the + sense finger, and CS− from the − finger:
  - 0.5 mΩ CSS2H-2512R-L500F on the drive group;
  - 1 mΩ CSS2H-2512R-1L00F on the converter group;
  - 2 mΩ CSS2H-2512K-2L00F on the rails;
  - 3 mΩ CSS2H-2512K-3L00F on the 54 V supply (the 5 mΩ is factory-order only).
- **R_VS = 100 Ω** in series with each TPS48111's VS pin, with its 100 nF + 1 µF after it,
  as TI recommends for noisy buses.
  - Hot-plugging a device onto a live group must not trip the fast detector, so use
    **XT90-S anti-spark plugs** on the 42 V devices.
- **Rail trips follow the converters (2026-11-19):** about 1.1× each converter's rated output
  (AM9032 24 V 10 A, AM3290 12 V 10 A, AM5942 5 V 15 A). The eFuse then latches off a
  sustained overload before the converter has to fold back on its own, and the rail reports
  why. Re-run `rails.py` if the 24 V or 5 V converter changes (OPEN, section 4.6).
  - The 5 V shunt is 1 mΩ (16 mV at 16.6 A). At 2 mΩ the trip voltage would have been
    33 mV, above TI's characterized 10–30 mV, and the 1.8 mΩ part is not stocked.
  - Every trip now sits inside 10–30 mV: 30 / 30 / 22 / 22 / 16 / 15 mV.
- **Rail input parts:** SMBJ26A / 13A / 6.0A TVS. The bulk caps are 2 × 47 µF / 35 V
  Panasonic EEU-FC1V470 (radial 6.3 × 11.2 mm, through-hole) on every rail, one part
  stocked at both TME and Mouser.
- **Rail short-circuit trip is disabled** (ISCP tied to CS−).
  - A hot-plugged device's input capacitors pull a spike of tens of µs. That would trip
    the 1.2 µs detector and drop the whole rail.
  - A real short behind a current-limited converter is still caught, by the over-current
    timer.
- **The 5 V over-current trip** at 16.7 A also protects the XT30 input from the 150 W
  converter, which could otherwise push 30 A through it.
- **Pre-charge current kept inside the FET's DC SOA (checked 2026-11-19):**
  - The IPB042N10N3G SOA (datasheet Diagram 3, T_C = 25 °C) allows about 2.2 A at 42 V for
    10 ms and about 0.8 A DC. The original 2 A ramps on the 42 V rails were outside it.
  - The ramps are now 0.4 A on the 42 V-input rails (both groups and the 54 V supply), about
    half the DC line. The 24 V rail ramps at 1 A (DC line about 2.2 A at 24 V). The 12 V and
    5 V rails keep 2 A, well inside.
  - The slower ramp needs a larger C_g, up to 1 µF on the drive group, rated 100 V
    because it charges to about VOUT + 12 V. The bootstrap cap follows TI's rule
    (≥ Qg / 1 V + 10 × C_g, up to 10.4 µF on the drive group), because the G pin draws its
    100 µA from BST for the whole ramp.
  - C_BST sits at 12 V, where an MLCC loses much of its value, so each rail uses
    10 µF / 50 V X7R 1210 caps (CL32B106KBJNNNE) counted at half value: three on the drive
    group, two on the converter feed, one elsewhere. The earlier 0805 / 25 V parts would
    have kept only about a third of their value at 12 V.
  - C_TMR is built from stocked 1206 parts, because 33 µF and 68 µF MLCCs are not sold:
    47 + 22 µF on the drive group (1.09 s), 22 + 10 µF elsewhere (0.51 s).
  - Start-up gets slower: the drive group takes about 0.4 s to ramp, after up to about
    2.3 s of initial bootstrap charging (nominal C; less with real DC-bias loss). Firmware waits on VSNS_OUT before switching INP.
  - Load capacitance is still *assumed* (OPEN D12): maxon does not publish the EPOS4
    input capacitance.
- **Brake chopper (decided 2026-11-19), sheet `brake`:**
  - When the EPOS4s brake, their energy goes back onto OUT42D. If the battery will not
    take it (full pack, cold cells, BMS charge cut), the bus climbs.
  - A TLV1805 comparator, with an LM4040 2.5 V reference and a divider of 178 k / 10 k
    plus 845 k of hysteresis, switches an IPB042N10N3G. That FET connects an off-board
    braking resistor (XT60) across OUT42D:
    - On at 47.5 V, off at 45.0 V.
    - Above a full pack (42 V).
    - Below the SMCJ45A (50 V minimum) and the EPOS4 50/5 (56 V absolute maximum,
      overvoltage cutout at 58 V).
  - It is hardware only and runs from OUT42D through a 12 V zener, so it works with the
    drive eFuse off: braking current then reaches OUT42D through the main FETs' body
    diodes.
  - The status signal BRAKE_ACT goes to ESP32 IO39. Firmware logs it and drops the drive
    group if the chopper stays active (a battery that keeps refusing charge would
    otherwise cook the resistor). Firmware also estimates the energy dumped into the
    resistor from BRAKE_ACT's on-time and the bus voltage (section 9).
  - Resistor: off-board, on a heatsink, sized to the braking power; 10 Ω / 100 W is a
    starting point (4.7 A at 47 V).
    - Sizing rule: 47.5 V / R must exceed the largest regenerative bus current, or the bus
      keeps rising until the EPOS4s trip on overvoltage (58 V). That trip is safe but stops
      the rover. The power rating only has to cover the average, because the chopper runs
      only while the battery refuses charge.
    - The on-board path is ready for down to about 4.7 Ω (10 A): the XT60, the
      IPB042N10N3G (about 0.4 W at 10 A) and the HV_Bus netclass on BRK_SW. The
      STPS3H100U freewheel diode only carries the cable-inductance current for a few
      microseconds after each turn-off.
  - Checked against the TLV1805 datasheet (SNOSD50A): pinout, SHDN low = run, 3.3–40 V
    supply, 100 mA push-pull gate drive. Power-on reset keeps the output high-Z while the
    supply comes up, and the 136 k status divider holds the gate low in the meantime.

### 4.4 54 V supply for the Zyxel XMG-105HP

- **What it powers:** the XMG-105HP, a 5-port 2.5G switch with four 802.3bt PoE++ ports.
  - It runs from an external 54 V adapter and produces the PoE itself, so the board only
    has to supply the voltage.
  - Its PoE ports are specified at 50–57 V, so 54 V is the target.
  - It draws 90 W at most, 70 W of which is the PoE budget.
- **Circuit (2026-11-20):** the 42 V converter group's output (OUT42C) → **LM5122**
  synchronous boost → 3 A fuse → 54 V output. Values are in [`rails.md`](rails.md). Until
  2026-11-20 the boost had its own input eFuse on the battery bus; see "Without its own
  eFuse" below.

| | |
|---|---|
| Controller | LM5122MHX/NOPB: 65 V operating input (75 V absolute max), 105 V switch node. MODE grounded = diode emulation, so no reverse current into the battery |
| Operating point | 30–42 V in, 54.35 V out, 100 W design, about 400 kHz (RT 22 k → 409 kHz), duty 0.26–0.47 |
| Power stage | Coilcraft MSS1583-223MED, 22 µH, Isat 10.4 A, above the 8.75 A maximum current limit; 2 × FDD86102 (100 V, 24 mΩ, DPAK, 19 nC gate charge); 10 mΩ WSL2512 current sense, cycle-by-cycle limit 6.5–8.8 A |

- **Review fixes (2026-11-19):**
  - The compensation network was drawn from COMP to GND, which suits a transconductance
    amplifier. The LM5122's error amplifier is a voltage-output op-amp, and TI connects
    the network between COMP and FB. As drawn the loop was effectively uncompensated.
  - The 1N4148W bootstrap diode (75–100 V) became a 200 V BAS21.
  - The 80 V bulk caps became 100 V parts.
  - The TMPA1265SP inductor (not at TME or Mouser) became the Coilcraft MSS1583-223MED,
    which also has a KiCad footprint.
- **Enable (BOOST_DIS):** Q_BEN holds the LM5122's UVLO pin low, which is shutdown, while
  BOOST_DIS is high. A 100 k pull-up and the expander's power-up state keep it high, so
  the boost is off until firmware clears it on expander P15.
  - Without it, the LM5122 would start at 27 V partway through the converter group's
    pre-charge ramp, and its load current would flow through that group's pre-charge FET.
  - Firmware enables the boost only once the converter group's main FETs are on; the boost
    then soft-starts in about 12 ms.
- **Without its own eFuse (decided 2026-11-20).** A boost has a direct path from input to
  output through the inductor and the high-side FET's body diode, so it cannot switch itself
  off or limit an overload that pulls its output below its input. What that means now:
  - **No separate off:** the Zyxel is on whenever the converter group is on. With BOOST_DIS
    set it sees about 0.7 V below the group's voltage, not 0 V.
  - **Hard short on the Zyxel cable:** current flows from OUT42C through the inductor and
    body diode; the converter group's eFuse trips (100 A ISCP), taking the Orin's converter
    down with it. Firmware reports it and can retry once the fault is gone.
  - **Overload below that:** the output fuse, a Littelfuse **0154003.DRT** (3 A slow-blow
    Nano2, 125 V AC/DC, 50 A breaking capacity) in an SMD OMNI-BLOK clip holder. 75 %
    derating gives 2.25 A, above the Zyxel's 1.85 A; it protects the 3 A JST-XH and its
    cable. A blown fuse is replaced by clipping in a 0452003.MRL, without soldering. No SMD
    PTC is rated for 54 V at 2 A; the 72 V radial PTCs are 18–21 mm discs, 24 mm tall.
  - **Telemetry:** the boost's input current is part of IMON 42V-C; VOUT 54V still reads
    the boost output. The former IMON_54, VSNS_SW54, INP_54 and INP_G_54 channels and the
    FLT_54 GPIO are spare (the GPIO keeps its 10 k pull-up as SPARE_IO38).
  - **Pre-charge:** the converter group's pre-charge now also charges the boost's output
    side and the Zyxel's input (ASSUMED 600 µF). At the same 0.4 A ramp it takes about
    0.21 s instead of 0.15 s, inside the FET's DC SOA; C_g grew, and with it the bootstrap
    rule asks for a third 10 µF C_BST on that group.
  - **Schematic numbering:** the dropped sheet's reference numbers (U7, Q19–Q21, R95–R111,
    JP6, D10 and so on) stay unused, so every other part kept its reference. Capacitors
    after the converter group moved up by one because of its extra C_BST.
- **Boost FETs need low gate charge,** not the switch D2PAKs. The LM5122 powers both gate
  drivers from an internal regulator fed from its input. With the FDD86102 that costs
  about 0.5 W in the controller; a 117 nC D2PAK would cost about 3 W.
- **No hardware over-voltage protection** in the LM5122. Firmware watches the 54 V output
  on the ADC and sets BOOST_DIS above 57 V, which drops the output to the input voltage;
  the TVS covers fast spikes.
- **Defaults ON:** the Zyxel is the rover's main network switch (decided).
- **Output connector (decided): JST-XH 2-pin,** used nowhere else on the board, so 54 V can
  never be plugged into a 5 / 12 / 24 V device.
  - XH is rated 3 A at 250 V with AWG 22, against at most 1.85 A at 54 V.
  - A short adapter cable goes to the Zyxel's DC plug.

### 4.5 Per-output PTCs

The rail eFuse trips at the whole rail's current. A fault that draws less than that, such
as 8 A through a thin sensor lead, needs protecting on its own output. That is the PTC's
job.

| Rail | PTC (Mouser) | Package | Rated | Hold / trip |
|------|-----|-----|-------|-------------|
| 24 V | YAGEO SMD2920B300TF/30 | 2920 | 30 V | 3 / 5 A |
| 12 V | MF-LSMF300/16X-2 | 2920 | 16 V | 3 / 5 A |
| 5 V | MF-LSMF300X-2 | 2920 | 6 V | 3 / 5 A |

- **Chosen 2026-11-19 for TME/Mouser sourcing.** Bourns has no 2920 part with 3 A at
  ≥ 30 V (its /24 V part would sit at its own voltage limit on a 24 V rail), so the 24 V
  outputs use YAGEO's SMD2920B300TF/30: 3 A hold, 5 A trip, 30 V, 40 A maximum fault
  current, about 12 mΩ. It replaced the radial MF-R300 the same day, so every PTC on the
  board is now SMD.
- **Hold current derates with temperature:** about 2.5 A at 50 °C and 2.25 A at 60 °C
  inside the rover (Bourns derating table).
- **Cable rule:** every device cable must carry the PTC's trip current continuously, so
  0.5 mm² (AWG 20) at least.
- **Devices that need more than 3 A** get a larger PTC on their output. This is a
  per-output BOM choice (OPEN D15).
- **No PTC on the 42 V outputs or the 54 V output:** the 42 V cables are heavy and no SMD
  PTC is rated for 42 V at drive currents, and the 54 V output feeds only one device behind
  its own eFuse.
- **A hard short on one output** browns out its whole rail for a moment. The converter
  current-limits (its rating sits just under the rail trip) and the PTC on the faulty
  output trips within a second or two and isolates it. If the rail eFuse latches first,
  firmware retries the rail; by then the PTC is hot and holds the short off. This is the
  accepted cost of sharing a switch per rail.
- **Output connectors (TME):**
  - 42 V: AMASS XT90PB-F.
  - 12 V: XT60PT-F (input XT60PT-M).
  - 5 V: XT30UPB-F (input XT30UPB-M). The vertical PCB XT30; KiCad's XT30U footprint
    was a cable part.
  - 54 V: JST B2B-XH-A(LF)(SN) (Mouser).
  - **24 V: Deans T, kept (decided 2026-11-19)** for the input and all eight outputs, so
    the existing 24 V harnesses keep working. This is the one exception to TME/Mouser
    sourcing (hobby shops), on the footprint copied from rev2. Check the footprint against
    the actual plugs before ordering.

### 4.6 Rail inputs

- **Converters (candidates 2026-11-19, AMPUL IP68 potted step-down modules):**

  | Rail | Module | Input range | Output | No-load current |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 12 V | AM3290 | 20–120 V | 12 V 10 A, 120 W | ≤ 48 mA |
  | 24 V | AM9032 | **35–60 V** | 24 V 10 A, 240 W | ≤ 60 mA |

  - **Pre-charge is fine with them:** none has an enable pin, but their no-load draw totals
    about 0.16 A. That stays inside the pre-charge FET's SOA while they wake up during the

    - A 10S pack spends its last stretch below that: 3.5 V per cell is roughly its last
      10–20 %.
    - Drive current also sags the bus. 60 A through about 0.1 Ω of pack and wiring is
      about 6 V, so the 24 V and 5 V rails could brown out during acceleration at mid
      charge.
    - The 12 V module (20–120 V) is fine.
    - Look for 24 V and 5 V modules that run down to about 25 V, or feed the 5 V
      converter from the 12 V rail instead.
  - The earlier 5 V candidate (AM1436) takes only 10–30 V and cannot sit on the 42 V group.

- **TVS:** standoff one step above nominal, for example SMBJ26A / 13A / 6.0A
  *(candidate)*. The clamp must stay under the rail FET's V_DS and the PTCs' rated voltage.
- **Bulk capacitance** at each rail input.

### 4.7 Protection layers

1. **Pack fuse + BMS** (harness): wiring and pack.
2. **Bus and rail TVS:** transients.
3. **42 V groups and 54 V supply short-circuit cut-off,** 1.2 µs: battery-side faults.
4. **Converter current limit:** the first thing a hard short on a rail meets.
5. **Rail eFuse over-current,** 0.5 s, latch-off: sustained overloads on a rail, and
   protection for the input connector and copper.
6. **Per-output PTC,** seconds: cable faults too small to trip the rail.
7. **FET over-temperature** through the TPS48111 DIODE input.
8. **Firmware policy:** retries, events, telemetry, user commands.

## 5. Control and telemetry

### 5.1 Switching

- **One TCA9535** 16-bit I2C expander (TSSOP-24) holds INP and INP_G for all five switches:
  10 lines, plus MOTOR_ARM and BOOST_DIS, with 4 spare. It keeps its outputs while the ESP32 resets, so an MCU crash or a
  reflash does not drop the rover (R4).
- **Power-on defaults (R4):** at power-on the expander pins are inputs, so each INP and
  INP_G gets a pull-resistor footprint.
  - **Default-ON rails:** INP_G is pulled up directly. INP is pulled up through an RC delay
    longer than the pre-charge ramp, so pre-charge always happens before the main FET.
  - **Rails behind a converter that default ON** are switched on before their converter
    has started. The converter's own soft-start then brings the rail up, which is why rev2
    worked once the FETs were bridged.
  - **Default ON (decided):** the 42 V converter group, the 12 V rail (Orin) and the 54 V
    supply (Zyxel). The rover then boots with a dead or unflashed MCU, and the Orin and its
    network come up.
  - **Default OFF:** the 42 V drive group (no controller means no use for the drives), and
    the 24 V and 5 V rails.
- **Firmware switch-on sequence, one switch at a time:**
  1. Set INP_G high.
  2. Wait until VOUT ≥ 90 % of VIN, read from the ADC, or time out at twice the expected
     ramp and report a fault.
  3. Set INP high.
  4. Set INP_G low.
- **Firmware boot rule:** read the expander state back. Never reset the outputs. Before
  switching a pin to output, write its current level, so it does not glitch.

### 5.2 Faults

- Each switch's FLT_I and FLT_T are wire-ORed into one line, which goes straight to an
  ESP32 GPIO with an interrupt. Firmware tells them apart from IMON at the moment of the
  fault.
- Fault lines are inputs only, so they don't need the expander's state retention.
- The switches latch off. Firmware retries by toggling INP: a few attempts, with
  back-off, then the rail is reported as faulted.

### 5.3 Current and voltage measurement

- **Two ADC128D818s** (8-channel, 12-bit, I2C, TSSOP-16, internal 2.56 V reference) use 14
  of their 16 inputs:
  - 5 switch currents (IMON, scaled to 2.4 V full scale).
  - 6 output voltages: one per switch, which show when pre-charge is complete and catch a
    failed FET, plus the 54 V boost output.
  - 3 rail input voltages (24, 12 and 5 V), through dividers.
  - 0x1D IN5 and 0x1E IN7 (the dropped 54 V eFuse's current and output) are tied to GND.
- **Configuration:** firmware must set mode 1 (Advanced Configuration register 0Bh). The
  power-on default, mode 0, converts only IN0–IN6 plus temperature, which would drop IN7 on
  both chips (VSNS_OUT_42C on 0x1D). VREF stays open for the internal reference, and
  V+ has the 1 µF + 100 nF the datasheet asks for.
- **Speed:** 12 ms per channel, so a full sweep runs at about 10 Hz. That is fast enough
  for telemetry and for watching a pre-charge ramp.
- **Board temperature (NTC) and the aux 5 V rail** go to ESP32 ADC1 pins (GPIO1, GPIO2).
- **42 V bus voltage** comes from the INA228.
- **One I2C bus** at 400 kHz carries all four devices, with 2.2 kΩ pull-ups at the ESP32 end (the
  routed bus is about 85 pF; section 8, I²C bus):

| Device  | Count | Addresses |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| ADC128D818 | 2  | 0x1D, 0x1E (A0 / A1 tri-level straps) |
| TCA9535 | 1     | 0x20 |
| INA228  | 1     | 0x40 |

### 5.4 Motor switch (decided 2026-11-19: hardware cut of the drive group)

- **Two separate cuts on the rover:**
  - **Battery E-STOP:** cuts the whole battery through the BMS soft-start pins, in series
    with the rover's on button. Nothing on this board is involved.
  - **Rear-panel motor switch:** cuts all motor power through this board's drive-group
    eFuse. It replaces EPOS4 STO as the motor cut, so the same wiring works whichever
    drives are fitted.
- **The cut:** the switch loop (JST-XH 2-pin, closed = motors allowed, so a broken wire
  means off) clears a 74LVC1G74 latch (SN74LVC1G74DCUT). Its /Q output (MOTOR_KILL) pulls the drive
  TPS48111's EN/UVLO low through Q_EN.
  - Low EN puts the TPS48111 into shutdown, and it actively turns the main and pre-charge
    FETs off (datasheet section 8.4). This is independent of the firmware.
  - The converter group, and with it the Orin, keeps running.
- **Clear path:** loop → 1 kΩ / 100 nF filter (MOTOR_OK) → 1 MΩ / 100 nF hold-off, with a
  Schottky across the 1 MΩ so opening the switch clears in about a millisecond → 74LVC1G17
  Schmitt buffer → /CLR.
  - The buffer is needed because the RC edge takes milliseconds, while the flip-flop allows
    at most 10 ns/V. A slow edge would sit in the input's linear region.
  - At power-on, the 100 nF is empty and the buffer holds /CLR low for about 100 ms, so the
    latch always starts off.
- **No restart when the switch closes:** the latch holds the drive group off until the
  ESP32 clocks it (MOTOR_ARM, expander pin P14).
  - Without the latch, EN coming back with INP still high would switch the main FETs
    straight onto discharged drive capacitors: no pre-charge, a large inrush, likely a
    150 A short-circuit trip.
  - Re-enable sequence in firmware:
    1. See MOTOR_OK high (GPIO14).
    2. Set INP_42D and INPG_42D low and clock MOTOR_ARM.
    3. Wait for the bootstrap cap (up to about 2.3 s).
    4. Raise INPG_42D and wait until VSNS_OUT_42D reaches the bus voltage.
    5. Raise INP_42D, then drop INPG_42D.
    6. Re-initialise the drives over CAN.
- **When the switch opens:** the hardware cut is immediate. The ESP32 also sees MOTOR_OK
  fall, drops INP_42D / INPG_42D, and stops sending drive commands.
- **Per drive family:**

  | | EPOS4 50/5 | CubeMars AK10-9 V3.0 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hardware enable | STO, which must now be tied permanently enabled | none: the supply cut is the only hardware stop |
  | Supply | 10–50 V, 56 V absolute max, cutout 58 V | rated 48 V; max not published (has OV protection) |
  | Current | 5 A continuous, 15 A peak (< 15 s) | 10.7 A rated, 31.9 A peak |
  | After a motor-switch cycle | reboots (unless +VC logic is fed separately); cold-start over CAN | reboots; cold-start over CAN |
  | Power / CAN connectors | XT90 drive output + CLIK-Mate CAN | XT30 2+2 (power and CAN in one plug), so the harness adapts from the XT90 and CAN outputs |

  - **Current limits:** four CubeMars actuators at 31.9 A peak would reach 128 A, against
    the 60 A / 1.1 s hardware trip. Configure each actuator's current limit so the sum
    stays around 55 A. With CubeMars, the firmware sustained-current limit (20 A for
    EPOS4) needs raising: four at 10.7 A rated is 43 A.
  - **Braking energy:** both families push braking energy back onto the bus. The brake
    chopper (47.5 V on / 45 V off) suits both; check CubeMars' overvoltage threshold once
    they publish it.

### 5.5 Soft power-off (decided 2026-11-19)

- **Goal:** switching the rover off with the latching button lets the manipulator stow
  first. Off is controlled by software; the battery E-STOP still cuts everything at once.
- **Wiring:** the BMS soft-start pair runs through the E-STOP to the board (JST-XH 2-pin).
  The latching button is **two-pole** (JST-XH 4-pin):
  - **Pole 1** shorts the soft-start pair. It is the only thing that can turn the rover
    on, because the board has no power while the rover is off.
  - **Pole 2** is a dry contact to the ESP32 (PWR_BTN, GPIO41: 10 k pull-up, 1 k / 100 nF
    filter, low = on).
  - The ESP32's hold, a Panasonic **AQY282S** PhotoMOS (60 V, 0.5 A, 2.5 Ω, isolated), is
    in parallel with pole 1. It is driven from GPIO40 (PWR_HOLD) through 330 Ω, about 6 mA,
    with a 100 k pull-down.
  - The E-STOP is in series ahead of both, so it always wins.
- **Why these parts:** the BMS is undocumented and probably needs a plain short. The
  PhotoMOS is an isolated contact, so the polarity and reference of the soft-start pins do
  not matter. One pole cannot do the job: with the hold closed, the button's state is
  invisible.
- **Sequence:**
  1. Button on → BMS on → the ESP32 boots and sets PWR_HOLD within about 0.5 s.
  2. Button off (PWR_BTN high, debounced) → the ESP32 tells the Jetson over UART.
  3. The Jetson stows the manipulator and shuts down, then replies "ready".
  4. The ESP32 drops the drive group, then releases PWR_HOLD. The BMS turns off.
  - Button back on before step 4 cancels the shutdown.
  - Timeout: release anyway 120 s after the button goes off, even without the Jetson's
    reply (for a crashed Jetson).
  - A reset or a dead ESP32 releases PWR_HOLD: with the button off, the rover dies, and
    with it on, nothing changes. The ESP32 task watchdog covers a firmware hang.
- **To check at bring-up:** measure the soft-start pins' open-circuit voltage and short
  current. The AQY282S covers up to 60 V and 0.5 A.

## 6. Controller

### 6.1 MCU: ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U

- **Soldered SMD module:** the radio is pre-certified and needs no RF layout from us.
  A chip-down ESP32 design is not worth the RF work.
- **The -1U variant** has a u.FL connector for an external antenna. That is needed because
  the board will sit inside the rover's body.
- **Why the S3 over the original ESP32:**
  - Native USB: flashing and debugging need no USB-UART chip, and USB stays available as a
    fallback host link.
  - Three UARTs, routable to any pin, for the direct host link.
  - A TWAI (CAN) controller.
  - BLE 5 and plenty of GPIO.
  - The catch: it has **no Bluetooth Classic** (see 6.2).

### 6.2 Xbox controllers

- Bluepad32 supports Xbox Wireless controllers on the ESP32-S3 **over BLE only**: models
  1708 and 1914 running controller firmware v5.x or newer.
- Older controller firmware uses Bluetooth Classic, which the S3 lacks. Update the pads
  with the Xbox Accessories app.
- If a Classic-only pad must work, use the original ESP32 instead. It has no native USB,

### 6.3 Host link: UART to the Orin

- ESP32-S3 UART1 runs straight to the Orin at 3.3 V logic, with no converter in between.
- UART0 is not used, because the ESP32 ROM prints its boot log there. Its pins
  (GPIO43/44) go to test pads as a debug console.
- **Connector:** a 4-pin JST-XH, B4B-XH-A(LF)(SN), the same family as the power-button header,
  carrying TX, RX, GND and
  one spare for RTS or a board-reset line.
  - Rev2's 2-pin screw terminal had no ground, so the link had to borrow its return path
    from the power wiring.
- **Protection:** 1 kΩ series resistors and a low-capacitance ESD array (USBLC6-2SC6,
  flow-through) on TX and RX.
  - The ESP32 is always on, but the Orin may not be. With the Orin off, an idle-high TX
    pushes at most about 3 mA into its unpowered header through 1 kΩ. With the original
    100 Ω it could have been about 30 mA, enough to partly power the Orin's I/O rail.
  - At 921 600 baud, 1 kΩ and the cable's tens of pF give an edge of about 50 ns, well
    under the 1.1 µs bit time.
  - Firmware also keeps TX low or high-impedance until the Orin's own TX is idling high.
- **Speed:** 921 600 baud by default. With five rails instead of 42 channels, a telemetry
  frame stays well under 1 kB, so 10–50 Hz telemetry plus gamepad relay leaves plenty of
  headroom.
- **Gamepad relay:** controller state travels in the same protocol. The ROS driver
  republishes it as `sensor_msgs/Joy`.
- **Orin side:** 40-pin header (J30).
  - Pin 8 is UART1 TX and pin 10 is UART1 RX, at 3.3 V. Ground is on pin 6.
  - On JetPack 6 the port appears as `/dev/ttyTHS1`. Enable it with `jetson-io` if it is
    not already set to UART.
  - The cable runs board TX to Orin pin 10 and board RX to Orin pin 8.
  - Confirm the pin numbers against NVIDIA's carrier-board specification before the cable
    is made.

**USB-C (kept):**
- USB 2.0 full speed, USBLC6-2SC6 ESD array (flow-through), 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs.
- **Receptacle: GCT USB4105-GF-A** (Mouser, LCSC), a USB 2.0 Type-C part with SMD signal
  pads and through-hole shell legs.
  - It replaced the through-hole USB4085-GF-A when the board went to 2 oz outer copper:
    the USB4085's 0.85 mm-pitch pins can only have a 0.15 mm annular ring, and JLC needs
    0.254 mm at 2 oz.
  - The footprint is KiCad's USB4105 one with the shell-leg pads widened to 1.15 mm (ring
    0.275 mm): `PowerBoard_rev3:USB_C_Receptacle_GCT_USB4105-GF-A_2oz`, made by
    `fix_footprints.py`. Pad names match the USB4085, so the schematic is unchanged.
  - The four corner signal pads are 0.05 mm shorter on the side facing the plastic
    locating pegs, which keeps them 0.24 mm off the peg holes (JLC 0.2 mm).
  - **Placement:** the receptacle's front face sits on the board edge (the footprint's
    "PCB Edge" line). A plug's overmold is larger than the receptacle and reaches below
    the board surface, so a face set back from the edge would stop the plug short.
- **Uses:** flashing and JTAG debugging through the S3's built-in USB-Serial/JTAG, and a
  fallback host link carrying the same protocol over CDC.
- **VBUS** feeds only the logic node, through a diode, for bench flashing (section 7). It
  never powers anything else.

### 6.4 CAN

- **Transceiver:** TWAI → **TCAN1044AV-Q1** (TCAN1044AVDRQ1, SOIC-8).
  - VIO 1.8–5.5 V, so it runs directly on 3.3 V logic.
  - ±58 V bus-fault protection, which matters when a CAN wire can touch 42 V.
  - CAN FD capable up to 8 Mbit/s; the EPOS4 runs classic CANopen at ≤ 1 Mbit/s.
- **Board hardware:**
  - Split 120 Ω termination on solder jumpers.
  - Two Molex CLIK-Mate 4-pin connectors for daisy-chaining, matching the EPOS4 50/5 X11 / X12
    pinout (1 CAN_H, 2 CAN_L, 3 GND, 4 shield), so maxon's CAN-CAN cable (520858) plugs in
    directly.
  - The EPOS4 terminates with DIP switch SW1 position 7.
  - TXD and STB have internal pull-ups, so a booting or reset ESP32 leaves the transceiver in
    standby, silent on the bus, until firmware drives STB low.
  - VCC comes from +5V_LOGIC: about 4.9 V normally and about 4.6 V on USB alone. Both are above
    the 4.4 V maximum undervoltage threshold, so CAN also works on the bench with USB only.
  - The CubeMars AK10-9 V3 carries CAN inside its XT30 2+2 power plug, so the harness splits CAN
    from the CLIK-Mate chain. EPOS4 (CANopen) and CubeMars (MIT / servo protocol) can share one
    bus at 1 Mbit/s as long as their node IDs and COB-IDs don't collide. Otherwise run them on
    separate buses, which needs a second transceiver because the ESP32-S3 has one TWAI.
  - There is no external TVS: the TCAN1044AV's ±58 V bus-fault rating covers a CAN wire
    touching 42 V, where a 24 V TVS would conduct.
- **Safety:**
  - The ESP32 produces a CANopen heartbeat, and each EPOS4 is configured to consume it.
  - The gamepad link is watched as well.
  - A dead MCU or a lost controller therefore stops the drives.
  - Drive authority (gamepad or PC) is explicit, with a handover and timeouts.

### 6.5 Boot and flashing

- BOOT and RESET buttons.
- Strapping pins (GPIO0, 3, 45, 46) are not used for I/O.
- Flashing and debugging go through the S3's built-in USB-Serial/JTAG.

## 7. Aux power (always on)

- **Buck (changed 2026-11-19): TI LM5017MR/NOPB.**
  - 7.5–100 V in, 600 mA, synchronous, constant on-time, SO-8 PowerPAD (DDA): easy to
    place, inspect and hand-solder, unlike rev2's 2 × 3 mm TDFN.
  - It is fed from the 42 V bus *before* the group switches and set to 5.29 V.
  - The LMR38010 it replaces is out of stock at Mouser until February 2027 and not sold by
    TME. The LM5017 is stocked at Mouser, and its 100 V input gives more margin over the
    SMCJ45A's 72.7 V clamp.
  - Values, from TI's datasheet (SNVS783K):

    | Part | Value | Why |
    |---|---|---|
    | RON (from VIN) | 150 k | about 390 kHz; on-time 206 ns at 72.7 V, above the 100 ns minimum |
    | Feedback | 33.2 k / 10 k | 1.225 V reference → 5.29 V |
    | Inductor | 33 µH Bourns SRR1260-330M | ripple 0.35–0.41 A; current limit 0.7–1.3 A |
    | Output | 3 × 22 µF / 25 V X5R 1206 (CL31A226KAHNNNE) | TI's 5 V example uses 3 × 22 µF |
    | Ripple injection (Type 3) | Rr 100 k from SW, Cr 3.3 nF to VOUT, Cac 100 nF into FB | 37–39 mV ramp on FB, above the 25 mV minimum; output ripple stays low with ceramic caps |
    | UVLO | 100 k / 6.8 k | on 19.2 V, off 17.2 V (20 µA hysteresis current) |
    | VCC / BST | 1 µF / 10 nF | as the datasheet asks |

  - **No diode emulation:** it stays in continuous conduction at light load. That is
    harmless here, because the SS34 OR diode blocks USB VBUS from back-feeding it.
  - **No power-good pin:** firmware watches +5V_LOGIC on AUX5_SNS instead.
  - **Load headroom:** 600 mA against a budget under 0.5 A. If Wi-Fi is ever used heavily,
    re-check the peaks.
- **5 V logic node:** a Schottky diode-OR of the 5.3 V buck and USB VBUS. It feeds the CAN
  transceiver's VCC and a 1 A, 3.3 V LDO (SOT-223) that supplies the ESP32, expander, ADCs
  and INA228. The buck's diode sits in the aux block and the USB one at the USB receptacle
  (layout, 2026-11-23).
- **Budget:** under 0.5 A at 5 V.
  - ESP32-S3 with BLE: roughly 100–150 mA, with peaks (more if Wi-Fi is used).
  - CAN transceiver: tens of mA when dominant.
  - Everything else: well under 50 mA.
- **Bring-up test (R1):** battery only, USB disconnected. 5 V and 3.3 V must be present and
  the ESP32 must boot.

## 8. Size and layout

- **Where rev2's 150 × 150 mm went:** 42 output connectors and 5 inputs, a row of 5 ATO
  fuses, D2PAK FETs, and the Pico module (51 × 21 mm).
- **What changes in rev3:**
  - The fuse row goes.
  - The Pico is replaced by a module of about 18 × 20 mm.
  - Six compact switches replace rev2's LT1910 stages, each a VSSOP driver plus D2PAK FETs.
  - Each output gets a 2920 or 1812 PTC next to its connector.
- **Connectors set the size floor.** Dropping the 15 V rail removes a column of eight XT60s.
  The output-connector field shrinks from about 100 × 85 mm to about 80 × 85 mm.
  - Add about 20 mm for the input-connector row and about 30 mm for the logic column.
  - The switch row needs about 35 mm: 12 D2PAKs across five switches, larger than the
    leadless FETs they replace.
  - The 54 V boost needs about 25 × 35 mm, with the inductor as its tallest part.
  - The target is about 120 × 140 mm, against 150 × 150 mm, to be confirmed after
    placement.
- **Stackup (decided 2026-11-19, revised the same day): JLCPCB 4-layer with 2 oz outer copper.**
  - 1.6 mm, JLC04161H-7628, **2 oz outer** and 0.5 oz inner copper, HASL lead-free, green
    mask, tented vias.
  - 2 oz outer copper is the one paid option. It halves the width the 30–40 A paths need
    (table below), which the 150 × 150 mm outline cannot otherwise give them.
  - Still 4 layers: a 6-layer board would add only thin inner copper. Blind and buried vias
    are paid extras with nothing to gain here.

  | Layer | Copper | Use |
  |-------|--------|-----|
  | L1 | 2 oz | Components; high-current pours |
  | L2 | 0.5 oz | Solid GND plane: unbroken, the return reference for I2C and the IMON lines |
  | L3 | 0.5 oz | 42 V bus and output planes under the L1 pours; rail backbones (24 / 12 / 5 V, 54 V); slow signals |
  | L4 | 2 oz | GND fill and return; logic and the aux supplies' SMD parts |

- **Board rules:** `kicad/rev3/gen/board_rules.py` writes them into the project and the
  board, with every limit taken from JLC's process for 2 oz outer copper.

  | Rule | Value | JLC limit (2 oz outer) |
  |------|-------|------------------------|
  | Track width | ≥ 0.25 mm (sturdy for hand rework; still fits 0.5 mm-pitch pins) | 0.15 mm |
  | Clearance | ≥ 0.15 mm (fine-pitch pads only; classes use 0.2 mm) | 0.15 mm |
  | Via | 0.9 / 0.4 mm (signals), 1.0 / 0.45 mm (power arrays), 1.2 / 0.6 mm (power classes) | drills under 0.3 mm cost extra |
  | Annular ring (vias, PTH) | ≥ 0.25 mm | 0.254 mm |
  | Hole to copper | via 0.25 mm, PTH 0.28 mm, NPTH 0.2 mm | 0.2 / 0.28 / 0.2 mm |
  | Hole to hole | vias 0.25 mm, pads 0.45 mm | 0.2 / 0.45 mm |
  | NPTH | ≥ 0.5 mm | 0.5 mm |
  | Copper to board edge | ≥ 0.3 mm | 0.2 mm |
  | Power-net tracks | ≥ 0.5 mm. The one exception is 0.25 mm inside an IC's "Pin escape" rule area (its pin rows + 1.2 mm), for the TPS48111's SRC pin on the output net | — |
  | Silkscreen | text ≥ 1.0 mm, lines ≥ 0.15 mm | 1.0 / 0.15 mm |
  | Solder mask dam | ≥ 0.1 mm; fine-pitch ICs get one opening over the pad row | 0.2 mm at 2 oz |
  | Blind, buried, micro vias, via-in-pad fill | not allowed | paid options |

  - **Netclasses:**
    - HV_Bus: VBAT, BUS42, the 42 V outputs, their drain rails and the brake switch node (BRK_SW).
    - HV_Power: the 54 V path and the boost's LIN and SW nodes.
    - Power: the 24 / 12 / 5 V rails, every PTC output node and CHG_N.
    - Supply and GND.
  - **Clearance is 0.2 mm in every class.** IPC-2221B asks 0.13 mm for 31–100 V under
    solder mask, and a larger class clearance would fail the 0.5 mm-pitch pins of the
    TPS48111 and INA228.
  - **42 / 54 V pours and tracks ≥ 1 mm get 0.5 mm on the outer layers.** This is close
    to the IPC 0.6 mm figure for bare copper.
  - **Power-class tracks must be ≥ 0.5 mm.**
  - **Hand-soldering rules:**
    - Courtyards keep ≥ 0.5 mm apart, so the iron fits between parts.
    - Pads in pours get thermal reliefs (0.5 mm gap, 0.5 mm spokes).
    - Power connectors, D2PAK/DPAK tabs, PTCs, 2512 shunts and inductors connect solid.
    - Teardrops are on for pads and vias.
  - **Zone checks:** KiCad's "minimum connection width" check is off (its default). It
    flags every pour-to-pad-corner neck; the 0.25 mm track minimum still covers tracks.
    Small pads may resolve with a single thermal spoke (`min_resolved_spokes 1`).
  - **Rule-file gotcha:** KiCad ignores the whole `.kicad_dru` without any warning if one
    constraint is malformed. `(constraint min_resolved_spokes (min 1))` did exactly that;
    the bare number `1` works. Check a rule change against a known violation, for
    example the VSSOP-8 fine-pitch clearance.
  - **Clean board:** silkscreen keeps 0.15 mm off pads. Courtyard overlaps, missing
    courtyards, silkscreen on copper, mask bridges and schematic mismatches are DRC
    errors, not warnings.
  - **Traceable:** the project defines `${BOARD}`, `${REV}` and `${FAB}` for silkscreen
    and fab-layer text.
  - **Order number:** none on the board. When ordering, choose "Remove order number"
    (free on standard orders at the time of writing; check the quote).

- **Current capacity** (IPC-2221, 20 °C rise, one layer; conservative for wide pours):

  | Current | 2 oz outer (chosen) | 1 oz outer | 0.5 oz inner |
  |---------|---------------------|-----------|--------------|
  | 3 A (one PTC output) | 0.4 mm | 0.9 mm | 4.7 mm |
  | 6.5 A (boost inductor) | 1.3 mm | 2.6 mm | 13.6 mm |
  | 15 A (5 V rail) | 4.1 mm | 8.3 mm | 43 mm |
  | 20 A (half of the 42 V input) | 6.1 mm | 12.3 mm | 64 mm |
  | 30 A (drive group) | 10.7 mm | 21.5 mm | 112 mm |

  - The inner 0.5 oz layers carry little current, so the heavy paths live on L1. L3 copper
    under them adds a little and spreads heat; via arrays tie the two only where current
    changes layer (budget roughly 1 A per 0.45 mm via).
  - The 42 V output strips are 7.4–7.7 mm wide on L1. At the full 30 A that is about a
    35 °C rise on L1 alone, only at the top of the strip: each XT90 takes its share on the
    way down, and the L3 plane runs in parallel.
  - **Solder-reinforced strips** remain a free extra if the 42 V strips run hot in testing:
    leave the mask open along them and flood with solder. Not planned for now.
- **Pads:** power-connector pads connect solid, with no thermal spokes.
- **Repeated switches:** all five switches share one hierarchical KiCad sheet, instanced
  five times with different values.

### 8.0 Floorplan (decided 2026-11-19: rev2's reading order)

- **Board:** 150 × 150 mm, rev2's outline and its five M3 holes: 3.5 mm in from each corner and
  one in the centre (75, 75), for the rover's middle standoff (restored 2026-11-21). The centre
  hole sits where the 24 V connector column meets the 12 V PTCs, so the 24 V and 12 V output
  columns start 6.5 mm lower than 5 V's (y = 81.5 mm), and their full-width output copper
  reaches down to them, so the 12 V current goes round the hole. Every hole is GND-plated
  (6.4 mm pad). Parts keep out of an 8 × 8 mm square on both sides at the corners, and of a
  7 × 7 mm one (just its courtyard; the placers add their gap) at the centre, where room is short.
- **Reads top to bottom, like rev2:**

  ```
  inputs   USB · BATTERY IN · CHARGER · PACK | 24V IN | 12V IN | 5V IN | POWER BTN, BMS SS,
  switches 42V drive + converter eFuses      | 24V    | 12V    | 5V    | MOTOR SW, ORIN UART
  ```

    XT60 and Deans T. These now use `Conn_01x02_MinusPin1`, a symbol whose top (+) pin is
    pad 2.

    that pad on the left.
- **Thermal: no heatsinks on the board.** Worst-case conduction loss, using
  IPB042N10N3G R_DS(on) at about 100 °C (7 mΩ):

  | Switch | Current per FET | Loss per FET |
  |---|---|---|
  | 42 V drive, 30 A design | 15 A (2 FETs) | 1.6 W; 0.7 W at the 20 A firmware limit |
  | 42 V converters, about 22 A | 11 A (2 FETs) | 0.85 W |
  | 24 V and 12 V, 10 A | 10 A (1 FET) | 0.7 W |
  | 5 V, 15 A | 15 A (1 FET) | 1.6 W |

  - Boost FDD86102: about 1 W each at 100 W. Shunts: under 1 W except R1 at a 40 A peak.
  - A D2PAK on about 6 cm² of copper on both outer layers, stitched with vias, runs near
    30–35 °C/W: about 55 °C above ambient at 1.6 W, far below the 175 °C limit.
  - Give the 42 V drive FETs and the 5 V FET the largest drain-tab pours on L1 and L4.
  - The brake resistor is off-board and does need a heatsink.
- **Outputs (decided 2026-11-19):**
  - 42 V: 3 drive, 2 converter feeds (one Y-split for the third converter).
  - 24 V, 12 V, 5 V: 6 each, against rev2's 8, 7 and 11.

  - Top: all connectors, the eFuse power parts, LEDs, bypass bridges, the through-hole

  - Bottom: each eFuse's TPS48111 and passives, directly under its FETs; the expander,

  - Column headers with the polarity in them ("+ 24V OUT 3A EACH −"), dividers between

- **Tools** (run in order, before any hand placement):
  0. `gen/rebuild_layout.sh --yes` runs the whole chain below, plus the schematic, BOMs and PDF.
  1. `gen/pcb_import.py` — imports the footprints, linked to the schematic.
  2. `gen/pcb_place.py --overwrite-placement` — this floorplan.
  2a. `gen/pcb_optimize.py` — simulated annealing over the small parts' positions and
     90° rotations. Small parts here means passives, small ICs and transistors, SMD diodes,
     LEDs and signal test pads; connectors, FETs, inductors, PTCs, electrolytics, bridges
     and the ESP32 stay put, and no part changes side.
     - **Cost:** net length (half-perimeter), weighted ×4 on switching, gate and
       Kelvin-sense nets. Each decoupling capacitor is also pulled toward the nearest IC
       pin on its rail (×3). GND and nets with more than 12 pads are left out.
     - **Legal at every step:** 0.6 mm between courtyards, through-hole pads block both
       sides, and parts stay off the holes, the logo spot, the column headers, the label
       spots and the test-pad label room.
     - **Result:** 20 million steps in about 3.5 minutes cut the weighted net length from
       about 16,900 to 8,250 (−51 %). The running total is checked against a full recount
       at the end. Since the templates (2g, 2h) place nearly everything, it moves only a couple of
       dozen parts. So it now takes 50 000 steps per movable part (2026-11-21): seconds instead of
       minutes.
  2b. `gen/power42.py` + `gen/pcb_power42.py` — the battery input and both 42 V blocks, designed by hand
     (placement in `pcb_place.py`, before the packer; copper after the optimizer). The parts are locked,
     and their copper areas are kept clear on both sides.
     - **Current path:** battery XT90 + → VBAT area (bulk caps, TVS; input ceramics under it on the
       bottom) → R1 upright → BUS42 bar across both blocks (L1 + L3, one stitching row at 3.2 mm
       pitch so the GND planes keep solid webs under it) → block shunt straddling the bar → drain
       area (FET tabs, bypass-bridge pad 1).
     - **Then through the FETs:** D2PAK source leads → narrow L1 output strip → about 15 vias per FET
       → full-width L3 output plane → output column → XT90 + pins, which sit straight below the leads.
     - **Charger:** its + comes into the bar on L3; CHG_N runs between the two XT60s on L3.
     - **Brake:** the resistor XT60 has its + under the drive XT90 + pins. The freewheel diode
       straddles the output strip and the switch-node patch, and the brake FET's source goes to a
       GND patch with vias.
     - **Escape vias:** every gate, both Kelvin pairs, the input-shunt Kelvin pair and the LEDs
       escape through vias whose spots on the bottom are reserved. They are routed on the bottom.
     - **Planes:** L2 is a solid GND plane; L4 has a GND fill.
     - The earlier automatic pours (`pcb_pours.py`, convex hulls around each net's pads) were
       removed: their shapes ignored the current flow and blocked the gate and sense routing.
  2c. `gen/power_rails.py` (copper also run by `pcb_power42.py`) — the 24, 12 and 5 V blocks, designed by
     hand in the 42 V blocks' pattern (2026-11-19). One entry per rail in `RAILS` (column edges and part
     references); the geometry is shared. The 24 V path is described below; 12 V is the same with VIN12 /

### 8.1 Hand soldering (decided)

- **Leaded packages only:** no QFN, DFN, SON, WQFN or TOLL.
- **Finest pitch:** the TPS48111-Q1 (VSSOP-19) and INA228 (VSSOP-10) at 0.5 mm. That is
  the same skill rev2's INA238 needed: drag-solder with flux and a fine tip.
- **Exposed pads** on the LM5017, the LM5122 and the ESP32 module's ground pad: put a
  1–1.5 mm plated hole in the middle of the land, so the pad can be soldered from the
  back of the board.
  - Rev2's 2 × 3 mm leadless MAX17541G had no such access. Bad joints there may have added
    to its "finicky" reputation, on top of the wiring error.
- **D2PAK and DPAK on large pours** need a high-power iron (≥ 80 W) or preheating, because
  the pours sink a lot of heat.
- **Passives:** 0603 minimum, 0805 where space allows.
- **USB-C:** the GCT USB4105-GF-A has 0.5 mm-pitch SMD signal pads. Tack the four
  shell legs first so the part cannot move, then drag-solder the pad row with flux and
  check for bridges under a magnifier. USB is only for flashing and debug; if it fails,
  the ESP32 can still be flashed through the UART0 test pads (TXD0 / RXD0) with BOOT held.
- **Test points for bring-up and debugging** (40 on the board):
  - **Every eFuse:** VOUT, IMON, FLT and GATE (trimmed 2026-11-20). EN/UVLO, INP, INP_G
    and VIN are probed on their resistors.
  - **Supplies:** both aux rails and the buck switch node and VCC, +3V3, and the boost
    input, switch node, VCC and output.
  - **Logic (trimmed 2026-11-20):** TXD0 / RXD0 (flashing and console fallback), the I2C
    pair (one bus to the INA228, both ADCs and the expander) and MOTOR_KILL (the safety
    output, otherwise only on a SOT-23 gate). The rest is probed where it already lands:
    UART and CAN on their connectors or the SOIC-8 transceiver, EN / IO0 on the RESET /
    BOOT buttons, VBAT / BUS42 / CHG_N on the power copper and XT60 pins, INA_ALERT, VBR,
    BRAKE_ACT, MOTOR_ARM / OK, PWR_HOLD / BTN and SS_A / B on their resistors and
    connectors. The latch's unused Q output is left open.
  - **Ground:** six GND loops (through-hole, for a scope or meter clip), one in each
    functional area. Signal test points are 1.5 mm SMD pads.
  - **Layout:** each eFuse's four pads stand in one column on the bottom beside its
    controller, the net name to the right of each. The ESP32's four have a field under the
    CAN block, and MOTOR_KILL sits left of the IMON ADC. The two supplies' pads are on the
    top, to probe with the board mounted. Keep them ≥ 2.5 mm from tall parts so a probe
    can land.

## 9. Firmware and ROS impact

- **New firmware** in `firmware/esp32/`, built on ESP-IDF or Arduino-ESP32, with Bluepad32
  and a CANopen stack such as CANopenNode.
- **Protocol v2** reports six branches: 42 V drives, 42 V converter feeds, 24 V, 12 V, 5 V
  and 54 V. Rev2 reported battery, 24, 15, 12 and 5 V.
  - It adds per branch: output voltage, pre-charge state and the latch-off fault reason.
  - It adds battery Ah/Wh, motor-switch state, brake-chopper activity (BRAKE_ACT, its
    energy and power estimate, and the resistor temperature estimate) and
    controller state.
  - **Protection logic that lives in firmware:**
    - Drive group: drop INP_42D on a sustained average above 20 A with EPOS4, about 45 A
      with CubeMars. The hardware trip is 60 A / 1.1 s.
    - Motor switch: the re-enable sequence in section 5.4.
    - Bypass detection: VSNS_OUT up with INP and INP_G low means a bypass link is fitted;
      report BYPASSED and stop sequencing that rail.
    - Boot: bring up the converter group, 12 V and 54 V in order (section 4.3). On every
      boot, first read each rail's VSNS_OUT and FLT. A rail the expander still holds on
      from before an ESP32 reset stays on, and nothing gets INP high before its output
      is up.
    - Brake chopper energy estimate (no extra hardware):
      - BRAKE_ACT (GPIO39) is high exactly while Q_BR conducts. Time its edges with MCPWM
        capture and sum the on-time.
      - While on, the bus is held between 47.5 V and 45 V; take V from VSNS_OUT_42D
        (about 10 samples/s) or use 46.25 V.
      - Power while on is V² / R_BRAKE, with R_BRAKE a configured value (10 Ω gives about
        212 W). Energy is that power times the on-time.
      - Accuracy about ±5–10 %, limited by the resistor's tolerance and its drift when hot.
      - Report the total energy (J), the power averaged over the last 1 s and 10 s, and the
        duty cycle. Log them so the brake resistor can be sized from real drives (OPEN).
    - Brake resistor protection: a first-order thermal model of R_BRAKE (thermal
      resistance and time constant from its datasheet and heatsink, configured). Warn at
      80 % of its rated temperature; drop the drive group at 100 %, or if BRAKE_ACT stays
      on continuously for several seconds. This replaces the plain "BRAKE_ACT stays on"
      rule.
    - Soft power: set PWR_HOLD at boot. On PWR_BTN off, run the Jetson stow-and-shutdown
      handshake, then release (120 s timeout; section 5.5).
    - Pre-charge sequencing: INP_G first, then INP only once VSNS_OUT has reached the
      input voltage.
  - Bump `jsonSchemaVersion` and `SUPPORTED_SCHEMA` together.
- **Transport:** UART1 at 921 600 baud, with USB CDC as a fallback; both carry the same
  protocol.
- **ROS driver:**
  - Small changes: still a fixed set of branches, now six with new names.
  - Add `sensor_msgs/Joy` for the relayed gamepad and Ah/Wh in `BatteryState`.

## 12. Cost: why per-rail (decided 2026-11-18)

Full breakdown and part numbers: [`cost.md`](cost.md), generated by `cost.py` from live
JLCPCB prices. The figures below are for a batch of 3 boards, counting only parts that
differ between options. Connectors, MCU, CAN, aux supply and battery monitor are the same
in every option.

Options A to B0 were compared with the old rail set (15 V rail, no 54 V supply). In that
comparison C cost $39. Since then:
- Dropping 15 V saved about $7.
- The 54 V supply added about $12.
- Splitting the 42 V group and moving to leaded parts added about $4: the second eFuse and
  the ADC128D818 cost more, and the D2PAK FETs are cheaper than the leadless ones.

| Option | What you get | Parts per board |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| **C (chosen)** | **An eFuse per rail with current monitor (two on 42 V), a PTC per output, no 15 V rail, the 54 V boost, all leaded for hand soldering** | **$48** |
| A  | Per-output eFuse everywhere (TPS48111, TPS1663, TPS25982) | $138 |
| A2 | Per-output, with the cheap TPS259470 wherever ≤ 5.5 A is enough | $114 |
| H  | A2, but the 42 V outputs share one switch, with a 58 V blade fuse per output | $90 |
| B  | A switch per rail, a blade fuse per output | $70 |
| B0 | A switch per rail, nothing per output | $26 |

**What C gives up against per-output switching:**
- No individual on/off per output, and no current reading per output: both are per rail.
- One faulty device browns out its whole rail until its PTC isolates it.
- A PTC reacts in seconds, not microseconds. That is still fast enough to protect a cable.

**What C keeps:** hardware protection on every output, a resettable fault path everywhere,
and the smallest board area of any option that protects each output.
